Nepal Floods: Over 600 Dead, 2,400 Missing After Glacial Collapse

Himalayan Flash Flood Wipes Out Villages Along Nepal-China Border

A catastrophic flash flood triggered by a glacial collapse in the Himalayas has devastated communities along the Nepal-China border. The death toll has climbed to 626 in Nepal, with 2,426 people still unaccounted for according to Nepal's National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority.

The disaster struck on August 26 when a massive chunk of ice plunged into a valley in Tibet, sending a devastating wall of water, mud, and debris through the Bhotekoshi and Trishuli river valleys. Entire villages, bridges, and roads were swept away, leaving survivors with nothing but the clothes they were wearing

More than 100 people are still trapped inside hydropower tunnels as rescue teams from Nepal, India, and China work together. India has sent an 11-member specialized team to assist with tunnel rescues while Canada and the EU have pledged millions in humanitarian aid.

Authorities have issued a new warning as a glacial lake formed upstream threatens to breach, potentially triggering a second wave of devastating floods. Rescue operations in the worst-hit Rasuwa district have been temporarily suspended, and people in low-lying areas have been ordered to move to safety.

Editor's Note

The Nepal flood tragedy has affected over 93,000 people. As a neighbor, India is closely monitoring the situation and providing continuous relief support .
